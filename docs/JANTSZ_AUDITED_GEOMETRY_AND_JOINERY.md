### Vocabulary used in this audit

- **Node or hub center:** the mathematical point where incident member axes meet.
- **Member or strut centerline:** the line joining two node centers.
- **Centerline chord:** the node-center distance; not a piece of wood.
- **Connection axis:** the direction an incident member approaches a node.
- **Shoulder:** the transition from the full timber section to a tenon.
- **Shoulder setback:** the axis distance between node center and shoulder datum.
- **Blank length:** the untrimmed timber length including any integral tenons.
- **Finished cut length:** a released shop dimension tied to a defined joint,
  datum, material condition, and tolerance. None is released here.

## 2. Excluded geometry studies - not build options

### Study 1 - regular tetrahedron geometry

A regular tetrahedron has:

- 4 nodes
- 6 equal edges
- 4 three-way joints
- 4 equilateral triangular faces
- 12 member ends

For edge length \(a\) and vertical height \(h\):

\[
h=a\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}},
\qquad
a=h\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
\]

For a true 36-inch vertical height:

\[
a=36\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}=44.090815\text{ inches}
\]

The prior 34.6-inch value was wrong. A 34.6-inch regular tetrahedron is only
about 28.25 inches tall.

At every node, the three member axes are noncoplanar and every pair has an
included angle of 60 degrees. "No hubs" is not a fabrication specification:
square timbers still require four resolved three-axis joints or another explicit
connection system.

This is an **unoccupied joint mock-up**. It can test fit, assembly order, and a
defined joint specimen. A tap or ring test does not establish connection
capacity, scaling, outdoor durability, or structural safety.

### Study 2 - dodecahedral geometry

"Half-dodecahedron" does not define a unique object. The cutting plane and the
retained vertices, edges, and faces must be named before a parts schedule exists.

#### A. Six-face dodecahedral cap

The cap made from one pentagonal face and its five neighboring faces has:

- 6 pentagonal faces
- 15 vertices
- 20 equal original dodecahedron edges
- 10 three-way nodes
- 5 two-way boundary nodes
- a ten-edge **skew, nonplanar** boundary

Derivation:

- Six pentagons supply 30 face-edge incidences.
- The patch has 10 internally shared edges.
- The boundary therefore has \(30-2(10)=10\) edges.
- Total unique edges are \(10+10=20\).
- Euler's disk relation gives \(V-E+F=1\), hence \(V=20-6+1=15\).

This is the only one-length dodecahedral cap considered here, but it does **not**
have a flat pentagonal base and it does **not** use 15 struts or six hubs.

#### B. Planar equatorial cut with an added base ring

**Mathematical comparison only - not part of the 12-foot project and not a cut
list.**

A center-plane cut perpendicular to a face axis is a different object. If its
cut points are connected by a regular decagonal base ring, it has:

- 20 vertices
- 30 members
- 20 three-way nodes
- 10 full dodecahedron edges of length \(a\)
- 10 cut half-edges of length \(a/2\)
- 10 base edges of length \(\varphi a/2\)
- a flat pentagonal crown face, not a single apex

It therefore has three member lengths, not one.

For full-polyhedron circumscribed diameter \(D\):

\[
a=\frac{2D}{\sqrt{3}(1+\sqrt{5})}
=0.3568220898D
\]

The former sentence \(a=0.714D\) confused diameter with radius. The former size
table happened to use the correct diameter factor:

| Circumscribed diameter | Edge \(a\) |
| ---: | ---: |
| 48 in | 17.1275 in |
| 72 in | 25.6912 in |
| 96 in | 34.2549 in |
| 120 in | 42.8187 in |

For the planar version with a **72-inch corner-to-corner base diameter**, a more
useful direct schedule is:

| Class | Count | Node-center length |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Full dodecahedron edge | 10 | 27.5016 in |
| Cut half-edge | 10 | 13.7508 in |
| Decagonal base edge | 10 | 22.2492 in |

Its geometric peak height is 30.6234 inches. This is not the former 45-inch,
15-member dome.

The base nodes in this planar cut also contain acute spatial relationships; they
are not represented by three mortises spaced 120 degrees around a flat triangle.
Pentagonal faces can rack unless the joint system supplies moment resistance or
the frame receives designed panels or bracing. "Self-locking" is withdrawn.

#### Immediate topology failure in the former kit

Fifteen struts supply 30 member ends. Six three-way hubs supply only 18 ports:

\[
2(15)=30\neq 6(3)=18
\]

The former assembly sequence also attempted to put five struts into a crown hub
with three mortises. The claimed geometry cannot be assembled.

Fifteen pieces at 25.7 inches also cannot come from four 8-foot boards:
\(15(25.7)=385.5\) inches exceeds the available 384 inches before any kerf.
Because each board yields only three such pieces, five boards would be the
theoretical minimum for that invalid schedule.

### Study 3 - full regular icosahedron geometry

A regular icosahedron has:

- 12 nodes
- 30 equal edges
- 20 equilateral triangular faces
- 12 five-way joints
- 60 member ends

For circumscribed diameter \(D\):

\[
a=\frac{2D}{\sqrt{10+2\sqrt{5}}}
=0.5257311121D
\]

For an 8-foot diameter:

\[
a=96(0.5257311121)=50.4702\text{ inches}
\]

The former 42.8-inch value was a dodecahedron value and was wrong for an
icosahedron.

## 4. Exact 2V connection-axis mathematics - not mortise geometry

> **Computational geometry only:** do not enter these values into a saw, drill,
> router, or CNC setup. Tool datums, hub solids, mortise roll, clearances, cutter
> radii, stock condition, and machining tolerances are unissued. Decimal places
> shown in this section describe the mathematical model, not shop accuracy.

Let hub center \(C_i\) connect to neighboring hub center \(C_j\). The member
length and the unit port axis leaving hub \(i\) are:

\[
L_{ij}=\lVert C_j-C_i\rVert,
\qquad
u_{ij}=\frac{C_j-C_i}{L_{ij}}
\]

For two incident members \(ij\) and \(ik\), their included spatial angle is:

\[
\theta_{jik}=\cos^{-1}(u_{ij}\cdot u_{ik})
\]

These vectors - not polygon interior angles and not a single drill setting - are
directions a future physical connection must accommodate.

### Chord inclination relative to the spherical tangent plane

For chord length \(c\) on sphere radius \(R\):

\[
\gamma=2\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{c}{2R}\right),
\qquad
\delta=\frac{\gamma}{2}
\]

Here \(\gamma\) is the central angle and \(\delta\) is the member's inward slope
below the local tangent plane.

| Member | Central angle \(\gamma\) | Inward tangent slope \(\delta\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Short | 31.7174744 deg | 15.8587372 deg |
| Long | 36.0000000 deg | 18.0000000 deg |

The former "31.7-degree and 58.3-degree drill angles" were not a hub
specification. The first number is a short-chord central angle; the second appears
in a tangent-plane azimuth relationship described below.

### Hub-local port coordinates

At an interior hub, let \(n\) be the outward radial unit vector and let \(e_x\)
and \(e_y\) be an orthonormal basis for the local tangent plane. A port with
inward tangent slope \(\delta\) and tangent-plane azimuth \(\phi\) has unit axis:

\[
u(\delta,\phi)=
\cos\delta(\cos\phi\,e_x+\sin\phi\,e_y)-\sin\delta\,n
\]

class.

### Member roll must also be defined

An axis alone does not orient a rectangular mortise. A reproducible natural roll
reference for port \(u_i\) is:

\[
r_i=\operatorname{normalize}\left(n-(n\cdot u_i)u_i\right),
\qquad
b_i=\operatorname{normalize}(u_i\times r_i)
\]

The ordered endpoint frame \((r_i,b_i,u_i)\) is right-handed. At the opposite end
of the same member, \(b_i\) reverses sign. An asymmetric wedge or key therefore
needs a separate member-wide sign convention before its roll is reproducible.

The final design must state whether the 1.25-inch tenon width and 0.5-inch
thickness align to \(r_i\) or \(b_i\). Both orientations still collide in the
current three-inch hub concept; changing roll is not a cure.

## 5. Finished timber length mathematics

Let:

- \(s_i\) = distance **along the member centerline** from hub center \(C_i\) to
  its shoulder-plane intersection
- \(s_j\) = corresponding centerline setback at the other hub
- \(t_i\) and \(t_j\) = actual tenon projections beyond the two shoulders

Then:

\[
L_{shoulder}=L_{ij}-s_i-s_j
\]

\[
L_{tip}=L_{ij}-s_i-s_j+t_i+t_j
\]

If setbacks and tenon depths are constant by hub class, the five fabrication
classes are:

| Member endpoints | Shoulder-to-shoulder length |
| --- | --- |
| Short 5-to-6 | \(L_S-s_5-s_6\) |
| Short 4-to-5 | \(L_S-s_4-s_5\) |
| Long 6-to-6 | \(L_L-2s_6\) |
| Long 4-to-6 | \(L_L-s_4-s_6\) |
| Long 4-to-4 | \(L_L-2s_4\) |

This is why subtracting two 1.5-inch tenons from a geometric chord is not a valid
cut-length calculation.

## 6. Why the proposed hub and tenon fail

The pasted kit proposed all of the following at once:

- a centered hub about 3 inches across flats
- a hub about 2 inches thick
- mortises 1.5 inches deep
- a claim that those mortises are through-cut
- tenons 1.5 inches long by 1.25 inches wide by 0.5 inches thick
- four, five, or six spatial port axes converging on one node

Those statements do not describe one solid.

If a member centerline intersects its port face 1.5 inches from the node center,
measured along the port axis, and its tenon enters 1.5 inches, the tenon reaches
the node center. Every port axis meets there, so every positive-area tenon and
mortise attempts to occupy the same central material. Independent through-tenons
cannot pass through that point without an explicitly designed interlocking
sequence.

The more favorable exact tangent-face interpretation still fails. Use a common
ideal port-face normal offset \(a=1.5\) inches, comparable to a three-inch
tangent diameter. This is a polygon apothem only where the port-face planes
actually enclose a suitable polygon; the four base planes span a 180-degree fan
and do not enclose a hub. Because the short and long axes tilt inward, their
exact node-to-shoulder setbacks are:

| Port | Node-to-shoulder setback | Tip distance after a 1.5 in tenon |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Short | 1.5593515 in | 0.0593515 in |
| Long | 1.5771933 in | 0.0771933 in |

Those tips are far closer than the tenon's smallest 0.5-inch dimension. An
oriented-box collision test using the exact port vectors gives:

| Hub | Colliding tenon pairs |
| --- | ---: |
| Five-way | 10 of 10 |
| Six-way | 15 of 15 |
| Four-way base | 6 of 6 |

Rotating every rectangular tenon 90 degrees does not help; every pair of the
unclipped test volumes still intersects. This test does **not** use the supplied
2-inch radial thickness to derive a closed hub solid, subtract mortises, or clip
the intersecting volumes. It proves the common tangent-plane tenon arrangement
is incompatible before those later solid-model steps.

Optimistic zero-clearance common-depth limits at the same 1.5-inch face offset
are:

| Tenon orientation | Five-way max depth | Six-way max depth | Four-way max depth |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1.25 in tangential by 0.5 in radial | 0.594 in | 0.332 in | 0.332 in |
| 0.5 in tangential by 1.25 in radial | 1.024 in | 0.916 in | 0.916 in |

Conversely, for this equal-offset, equal-depth oriented-box model and either of
the two stated 90-degree rolls, the 1.5-inch tenons first become collision-free
at these approximate common face offsets:

| Tenon orientation | Five-way offset | Six-way offset | Four-way offset |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1.25 in tangential by 0.5 in radial | 2.371 in | 2.611 in | 2.611 in |
| 0.5 in tangential by 1.25 in radial | 1.958 in | 2.061 in | 2.061 in |

Doubling those offsets gives a 3.92- to 5.22-inch tangent-diameter comparison
scale **before** adding clearance, cutter radii, wood ligaments, wedge expansion,
grain requirements, or structural edge distances. It is not literal
across-flats for every hub. Other roll angles, unequal offsets, and staggered
depths were not globally optimized. These are collision-only model results, not
approved hub sizes.

The collision setup is reproducible. For inward tangent slope \(\delta\), tenon
length \(\tau\), width \(w\), thickness \(t\), exact port direction \(u\), and
member roll axes \((b,r)\):

\[
h=\frac{a}{\cos\delta},\qquad
c_{box}=(h-\tau/2)u
\]

Each oriented tenon box uses axes \((b,r,u)\) and half-extents
\((w/2,t/2,\tau/2)\). Swapping \(w\) and \(t\) produces the tested 90-degree
roll. Pair intersections were evaluated with the oriented-box separating-axis
test.

A useful conservative **sufficient clearance** screen for two centered tenons
is:

\[
d\ge\frac{r_b}{\sin(\theta/2)}
\]

where \(d=s-\tau\) is the tenon-tip distance from the node center, \(s\) is the
axial shoulder setback, \(\tau\) is tenon length, \(\theta\) is the
included port angle, and \(r_b\) is a radius enclosing the tenon cross-section.
For the proposed 1.25 by 0.5-inch rectangle:

\[
r_b=\tfrac12\sqrt{1.25^2+0.5^2}=0.673146\text{ inches}
\]

Using the minimum 2V neighbor angle of 55.5690118 degrees gives a conservative
screening distance of 1.4441 inches between the tenon tip and node center. With a
1.5-inch embedment, the corresponding **axial shoulder setback** is about
2.9441 inches. Its comparable tangent-plane normal offset is about 2.80 inches
for long ports to 2.83 inches for short ports, versus the proposed 1.50-inch
offset. Equivalently, the tangent-diameter comparison is about 5.60 to
5.66 inches rather than 3 inches. This is only a screening bound;
failing it does not by itself prove collision. The oriented-box test above does.
The final hub still needs exact solid subtraction and ligament checks.

The first three-inch hub layout therefore does not fit. This is a tenon-volume
geometry failure, not a failure of the dome centerlines. A larger monolithic hub
could still fail structurally because one grain direction cannot favor every
spatial mortise and because cross-grain ligaments can split.

The phrase "1.5-inch-deep through-mortise in a 2-inch block" is not a valid
through-mortise specification. Overall block thickness does not name a local
entry/exit path. A through path must identify its entry face, exit face, and full
distance along the mortise axis; none was defined.

Between hypothetical parallel tangent support planes three inches apart, the
axis path is about 3.119 to 3.154 inches. A pentagon or the base port-face fan
does not necessarily supply such an opposite exit face. Under a separate
assumption of parallel radial planes two inches apart, traversing the radial
thickness along these nearly tangent axes would take about 7.319 inches for a
short port or 6.472 inches for a long port. None of the examined canonical paths
is 1.5 inches.

The face ligaments are also only geometric remnants, not verified edge
distances. At a 1.5-inch apothem, a centered 1.25-inch mortise leaves about
0.465 inch on each side of a regular pentagon face, but only about 0.241 inch on
each side of a regular hexagon face. Wedge action and real cutter radii reduce
the usable material further.

## 7. Joinery claims withdrawn from the pasted content

The following must not appear as specifications:

- "strongest all-wood joint" or star-based strength rankings
- "maximum strength" or "self-locking under compression"
- approximately 100 psi wedge pressure
- 10-, 50-, or 100-year life claims
- "the joint tightens as the wood shrinks"
- zero-clearance equal tenon and mortise dimensions
- independent plus-or-minus tolerances that can stack into interference
- a universal 12-degree wedge angle
- universal CNC accuracy, price, or turnaround promises
- a one-weekend structural build promise
- scaling a 6-foot test directly to a 12-foot occupied structure
- a tap or audible ring as proof of structural adequacy

Wood is anisotropic and moisture-responsive. Joint behavior depends on species,
grade, grain direction, defects, section loss at mortises, bearing and shear
areas, moisture, exposure, fit, adhesive, retention, and load direction. A
historical precedent is not a capacity calculation for this geometry.

The named liquid hide glue is not acceptable as a generic backyard structural
adhesive. Its own manufacturer states that it is not for exterior, moisture-
prone, structural, or load-bearing use.

The former table-saw and router steps are also withdrawn as shop instructions.
They omitted guarding, workholding, cutter limits, kickback control, dust control,
and machine-specific procedures. Tenon length is established by the shoulder
location; it is not created by setting a saw blade 1.5 inches high.

## 8. What a prototype can and cannot establish

A documented joint prototype can establish:

- whether the chosen connection can be assembled
- whether its jig reproduces the CAD port axes
- whether actual stock fits the chosen clearance at a recorded moisture content
- where solid collisions, breakout, or insufficient ligaments occur
- the failure mode and load of that specimen under a defined test setup
- acoustic response under a defined measurement setup

Any destructive or load test needs a written test plan, controlled apparatus,
an exclusion zone, recorded specimen and material conditions, and qualified
supervision. One specimen result is not a design value.

It cannot by itself establish:

- full-scale member or connection capacity
- wind, uplift, snow, seismic, anchorage, or foundation performance
- outdoor durability or fire performance
- public or occupied safety
- code compliance
- that an audible ring indicates structural adequacy
- that a small model scales directly to the 12-foot dome

This document does not authorize anyone to enter, sit beneath, display as
occupiable, or permanently install a mock-up. Occupancy requires a separate
signed structural/fabrication release and applicable local approval.

## 9. Inputs required before fabrication CAD resumes

The next joint model needs these decisions and measurements:

1. Actual dressed strut width and height, measured at the intended service
   moisture content.
2. Strut species, structural grade, defect limits, and grain requirements.
3. Hub material system: monolithic, laminated, split, or multi-part.
4. Whether the all-wood/no-metal requirement is absolute.
5. A joint and retention family compatible with the actual section. A standard
   pegged mortise-and-tenon is unavailable in the current 1.5 by 1.5-inch stock;
   a different family or larger stock requires a new engineering review.
6. Whether the structure must be demountable and whether wedges must remain
   serviceable.
7. Minimum hub ligament and edge-distance criteria established by engineering or
   a defined destructive test program.
8. Exterior exposure, drainage, finish, and adhesive system.
9. Design loads, site conditions, anchorage, foundations, openings, and intended
   occupancy.
10. Available tooling and the shop's measured machining capability.

Those inputs are necessary but not sufficient. They permit a new hub study to
begin. Finished cut dimensions or printable fabrication drawings additionally
require documented connection review, structural/load-path review, accepted
prototype/test evidence, and an explicit fabrication release.

## 10. Wood panels, entrance, and platform spatial studies

The canonical shell contains 40 exact planar node-center face references in two
families:

- 30 P1 isosceles faces: 39.350380, 39.350380, and 44.498447 inches;
  44.498447-inch base by 32.456501-inch planar altitude; 722.131952 square
  inches each.
- 10 P2 equilateral faces: 44.498447 inches on every side;
  38.536786-inch planar altitude; 857.413562 square inches each.
- Total gross node-center face area: 30,238.094167 square inches, or
  209.986765 square feet, with no waste allowance.

These are mathematical face references, not finished panel cuts or a purchase
quantity. Thickness, timber underlap or reveal, corner relief at hubs, edge
bevels, attachment, gaskets, drainage, moisture allowance, grain direction,
fire behavior, weathering, and acoustic performance are unresolved.

The optional entrance study opens one contiguous six-face patch: F006, F007,
F012, F021, F022, and F023. It hides member references L-03, L-06, S-11, S-12,
L-16, L-17, and L-26 and hides node V007 only in that presentation. The

A schematic raked wood cassette shows a **36-inch clear width by 58-inch clear
rise**. This is a crouch entrance, not standing-height access. The 65-member,
26-node, 40-face canonical reference and its exports remain unchanged.

The entrance cassette does not establish a replacement load path, member or
joint size, reinforcement, door, threshold, weather seal, drainage, egress,
guard, foundation connection, or acoustic behavior. It must not be treated as a
fabrication detail.

The separate 192-inch corner-to-corner platform is an **unengineered spatial
concept**. Its regular 96-inch-radius decagon edge is:

\[
2(96)\sin(18\text{ degrees})=59.331263\text{ inches}
\]

The corresponding platform and dome vertices differ in radius by 24 inches.
That does **not** create a uniform 24-inch apron: the perpendicular separation
between corresponding flats is only
\(24\cos(18\text{ degrees})=22.825356\) inches. The CAD separates 35 deck-board
envelopes, 10 rim pieces, 10 primary radial frame lines, one center study block,
and 10 supports located on the dome base-node axes. The 36-inch approach aligns
with the optional entrance cassette. The platform retains an all-wood connection
goal, but no platform joint dimensions are released. The display does not
establish a joist schedule, deck capacity, connection, foundation, guard,
egress, or shared dome load path.

The former 37, 55, 73, 110, and 180 Hz claims, isolation frequency, frequency
boost, tuned-column, instrument-top, sounding-board, and parabolic-reflector
statements are withdrawn. The audited dome has no defined sealed cladding or
acoustic boundary. A solid deck does not make the dome air and open under-deck
space one Helmholtz chamber. Deck orthotropy, damping, connections, supports,
loads, openings, leakage, absorption, and measurement conditions are undefined.
A geodesic hemisphere is not a parabolic reflector.

The verified dome model does not validate the platform. Keep the platform
structurally independent unless and until a coordinated structural design says
otherwise. Acoustic claims require a defined boundary model, modal analysis,
and measured prototype evidence.

## 11. References used to set the engineering boundary

- [Timber Frame Engineering Council - Standard for Design of Timber Frame Structures, TFEC 1-2019](https://www.tfguild.org/timber-frame-engineering-council/standards/view/104/download)
  supplies the wood-peg design range and minimum tenon, edge, and end-distance
  geometry used to screen the modeled 1.5-inch section.
- [USDA Forest Products Laboratory - Fastenings](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/7144)
  explains that connection design must account for wood strength along and
  across grain and dimensional change with moisture.
- [USDA Forest Products Laboratory - Wood as an adherend](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/9067)
  describes wood as hygroscopic and orthotropic and identifies species and
  within-species variation as material to bond performance.
- [American Wood Council - wood peg and mortise-and-tenon design resources](https://awc.org/faq/where-can-i-find-information-on-wood-dowel-wood-peg-design-values/)
  points designers to dedicated research rather than a generic rule of thumb.
- [ASCE - Strength and Stiffness of All-Timber Pegged Connections](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%290899-1561%282009%2921%3A1%2810%29)
  reports that traditional all-timber connection design requires strength and
  stiffness models supported by testing.
- [Titebond Genuine Hide Glue limitations](https://www.titebond.com/print/product/9e9995b4-08eb-4fc6-8254-c47daa20f8ed)
  expressly exclude exterior, moisture-prone, structural, and load-bearing use.
- [OSHA router guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/woodworking/production/machines-tools/routers)
  and [OSHA table-saw guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/machine-guarding/saws/table)
  identify guarding, tool projection, point-of-operation, flying-chip, and
  kickback hazards omitted from the pasted shop sequence.
